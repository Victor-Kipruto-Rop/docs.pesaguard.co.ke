Security

Data protection Live

PesaGuard's backend includes redaction and field-protection helpers, audit-detail sanitization, and a versioned retention policy. The policy describes target handling; operators must confirm that scheduled cleanup, archive lifecycle, approvals, and legal holds are enabled in the deployed environment.

Data categories and policy periods

The backend repository's retention policy is marked as requiring approval. Its declared values are below; they are policy configuration, not evidence that every storage system has enforced deletion on schedule.

CategoryDeclared retentionPolicy notes
Transactions2,555 daysPostgreSQL hot storage, object-storage warm tier and cold archive; archive before controlled tenant-scoped purge and legal-hold check.
Audit data2,555 daysAppend-only store and immutable archive; purge only after archive verification and legal-hold check.
Application logs90 daysCentralized log storage with lifecycle expiration after archive or export.
Security logs730 daysRestricted store and immutable archive; controlled purge after incident and legal-hold review.
Raw provider data2,555 daysRestricted object-storage raw zone and cold archive; verify archive integrity before lifecycle deletion.
Analytics1,825 daysCurated storage and derived metric tables; derived data is rebuildable subject to source-retention review.
User data365 daysPostgreSQL with selected sensitive fields encrypted; tenant offboarding uses anonymization or approved erasure.

Minimization, encryption and redaction

Only collect and retain fields required for reconciliation, operations, fraud review, support, and legally approved evidence. The payload-protection helper encrypts a defined set of sensitive JSON leaf fields; provider configuration serialization masks credential-like keys; and audit detail sanitization redacts recognized secret values and keys while bounding the size and depth of audit details.

These protections are path-specific, not a promise that every log statement or export is automatically scrubbed. Before adding a data field, identify its classification, purpose, access roles, retention class, export path, and deletion behavior. Never place access tokens, passwords, signing secrets, or full payment credentials in audit details or routine logs.

Access, exports and data-subject requests

  • Use tenant-scoped roles for queries, reports, exports, and administrative tools. Limit operational access to the people who need it and review that access periodically.
  • Exports should contain only requested fields, preserve tenant boundaries, and be protected during transfer and storage. Avoid downloading production data to unmanaged devices.
  • Handle deletion requests through the approved tenant-scoped workflow. Check legal holds, archive obligations, downstream copies, derived analytics, and backup expiry before confirming erasure.
  • Record approvals and outcomes without copying the sensitive records into the approval ticket or audit description.

Operational verification

  1. Obtain approval for the retention policy and map each listed category to every live database, object store, log sink, queue, export store, and backup.
  2. Confirm each retention and archive job is scheduled, has least-privilege access, emits observable success/failure signals, and can be safely rerun.
  3. Test legal-hold and approval gates before a purge; verify that tenant-scoped deletion cannot affect another tenant.
  4. Inspect representative logs, audit entries, provider responses, and exports for sensitive values using synthetic test data.
  5. Run a restore/deletion exercise to confirm archives, backups, and downstream replicas follow the intended retention and recovery policy.

The retention source is retention_policy.json; application-level protection and audit sanitization are implemented in data_protection.py, provider_management_service.py, and action_audit.py. These files do not, by themselves, prove deployment configuration or deletion completion.
