Risk operations · Investigation

Risk alerts, anomaly signals and fraud review

Risk analytics helps prioritize investigation; it should not silently convert a model score or anomaly into a definitive fraud verdict. This guide explains alert triage, evidence review and operational controls.

Use the right PesaGuard surface: customers operate the product in app.pesaguard.co.ke; developers manage available integration workflows in developers.pesaguard.co.ke; requests go to the documented API at api.pesaguard.co.ke. Confirm actual endpoints and supported behavior in the OpenAPI contract.

1. Signal, alert and finding are different

  • Signal: a rule or analytic indicates unusual behavior relative to its configured criteria.
  • Alert: a signal is routed to a person or system for review under an alerting policy.
  • Investigation: an authorized reviewer gathers contextual evidence and checks for benign explanations.
  • Finding/disposition: a documented decision made under the organization's process.

A high score or unusual transaction is not proof of fraud by itself. The exact scoring model, rules and alert actions available depend on the deployed product configuration.

2. Triage an alert

  1. Confirm the alert's tenant, source, event time, detection time and affected transaction reference.
  2. Read the rule name/version and explanation available in the alert details.
  3. Check source completeness and data freshness; missing records can create misleading anomalies.
  4. Compare transaction amount, frequency, account/reference patterns and recent related events when authorized.
  5. Check whether duplicate callbacks, retries, reversals or legitimate batch activity explain the pattern.
  6. Assign a priority based on policy and potential impact, not only on color or score.
  7. Record the disposition and supporting rationale in the approved workflow.

3. Evidence a useful alert should carry

  • Stable alert and transaction identifiers.
  • Signal category, rule/model version, score and severity when those values exist.
  • Event time, detection time, source and tenant context.
  • Explainable triggered conditions or reason codes, not only an opaque score.
  • Relevant evidence references and related events, with sensitive values masked.
  • Current review status, authorized owner and audit history if supported.

4. Severity and escalation

Define severity using documented criteria such as potential impact, confidence, exposure and time sensitivity. Establish who can acknowledge, investigate, escalate, close or override an alert. Ensure that escalation has an owner and response expectation. Do not make unsupported claims about guaranteed detection coverage or a specific false-positive rate without measurement evidence.

5. Manage false positives and model drift

  • Capture a reason for benign dispositions using a controlled taxonomy.
  • Measure false-positive rates on reviewed cases with a defined denominator and review window.
  • Track alert volume by rule version, source, transaction type and time.
  • Monitor changes in source volume and schema that can shift score distributions.
  • Use a controlled approval process for threshold/rule changes and preserve the prior configuration.
  • Evaluate performance on representative, time-separated data and document known blind spots.

6. Human review and audit

High-impact actions should require the appropriate role and, where policy requires, a second-person review. Record who acted, when, what changed, the reason and the evidence consulted. Avoid deleting an alert to make metrics look better; use explicit dispositions and keep the event history available to authorized auditors.

7. Data protection

  • Limit alert detail to users with a legitimate operational need.
  • Mask account and personal identifiers by default.
  • Never include API keys, passwords, signing secrets or full raw payment payloads in alert messages.
  • Apply documented retention, access logging and secure export controls.
  • Do not use a risk signal as the sole basis for a high-impact customer decision without the legally and operationally required review.

8. API and Dashboard boundaries

Review alerts in the authenticated Customer Dashboard when the feature is available. External systems should consume only documented risk operations and supported event types. Manage developer access through developers.pesaguard.co.ke; verify exact operations in the OpenAPI contract and call api.pesaguard.co.ke. Do not invent a risk endpoint or assume every alert is delivered by webhook.
